# Answer guide

This is a conceptual reasoning key, not packet findings. More than one defensible answer can pass. The blockquotes are exact narration anchors, not evidence that an event occurred.

> We keep observed events separate from assumptions.

> A capture shows traffic from one viewing point. It cannot automatically tell us everything that happened elsewhere.

A response passes when facts trace to the prompt, explanations are hypotheses, the source-specific limitation and unknown are named, prohibited inferences are avoided, and the next check is authorized or the status is explicitly unresolved.

### INTRO-01
> Your network capture shows an unfamiliar connection. But that pattern might be an ordinary software update.

**Reasoning key:** Contact is the observation. An update is plausible, not proven. Malicious purpose is unsupported. Check authorized context or remain unresolved.

### INTRO-02
> An incomplete capture may leave a gap where you want certainty. We'll treat that gap as a limitation.

**Reasoning key:** Do not fill the gap or infer a hidden sequence. Seek authorized evidence covering it or remain unresolved.

### INTRO-03
> Encrypted traffic can still provide useful context, but seeing a connection doesn't reveal every message or which program made it.

**Reasoning key:** Report connection context, not message content or program identity. Use authorized additional records or remain unresolved.

### INTRO-04
> Repeated connections may deserve a closer look, but monitoring software can make them too. Timing alone can't establish their purpose.

**Reasoning key:** Monitoring remains an alternative. Repetition or timing supports review, not a claim about purpose.

### INTRO-05
> Connections inside a network pose a challenge: traffic can show systems in contact but not who authorized the activity.

**Reasoning key:** Report contact, not authorization or actor. Authorization requires authorized additional evidence.

### INTRO-06
> A large outbound transfer may be concerning, but its size doesn't identify the data or prove theft.

**Reasoning key:** Report the large transfer, not its content or theft. Check authorized relevant records or remain unresolved.

### INTRO-07
> A suspicious download raises one question about how it arrived and another about what happened afterward. Those aren't the same claim.

**Reasoning key:** Arrival and later activity are separate claims. A later-activity claim needs separate authorized evidence.

## Reflection key
A supported unresolved conclusion avoids a false accusation, identifies missing evidence, and directs the next authorized check.

> The goal is a conclusion you can defend. It should explain the traffic and clearly say what remains unknown.
