## Assessment and alternative tests

A checked narrow claim is: **this authored capture shows a DNS question/answer and an outbound, two-way cleartext HTTP request/response between the cited documentation addresses at the cited UTC times** (frames 1–2 and 3–9). Decoded `GET /status` and HTTP response code `200` in frames 6 and 8 make **Web Protocols (T1071.001) under Application Layer Protocol (T1071)** a *possible protocol-level mapping*. ATT&CK places it under the **Command and Control tactic**; that taxonomy does **not** establish an adversary, command-and-control activity or why the exchange occurred. The fixture has no TLS in the logged `frame.protocols` values, so encryption does not conceal this particular HTTP exchange. In a different encrypted capture, an address, port or TLS alone may leave web-protocol behavior unresolved; do not import this fixture’s decoded result.

The parsed `supplemental-context.csv` has one record, `CTX-01`, associating scenario asset `LAB-WS-01` with `192.0.2.10`, a *possible* `scheduled_software_status_check` and planned peer `service.academy.test`. Its planned window, **2025-04-08T09:25:00Z–09:35:00Z**, overlaps the packets, but `record_status=planned_not_execution` and `evidence_scope=not_packet_or_process_or_owner_approval`. A routine updater/business-service explanation predicts an actually running, approved process and owner-confirmed destination/schedule; the calendar does **not** provide those confirmations. A suspected command-and-control explanation needs distinguishing, bounded context—for example a host-and-time-matched unapproved initiating process or further relevant behavior—not simply this HTTP exchange or an unfamiliar peer. The current packets fit both possible purposes. Do not infer the initiating process, real-world peer ownership, human intent or authorization from a DNS answer, Host header, calendar label or ATT&CK name; do not infer absence of other exchanges outside this synthetic 0.3-second file.
