# Answer Guide

No case data is supplied, so this key assesses reasoning and structure rather than prescribing a finding. Mark unavailable evidence `unresolved` and name the conclusion it weakens; unresolved status does not justify unsupported claims or missing required portfolio components.

- [ ] **Seven-artifact consistency:** The executive summary, technical report, timeline, evidence index, playbook, detection proposal, and revision log all match the revised conclusion, including an inconclusive one.
- [ ] **Decision usefulness:** The summary states the finding, confidence, affected scope, decision, owner, and impact without treating an encrypted transfer as proof of data loss.
- [ ] **Defensible analysis:** The report begins with the tested question and scope; separates observation, corroboration, and inference; compares a harmless explanation; and bases confidence on distinguishing evidence and limits. It states captured traffic direction and time period, truncation, encryption and missing-record limits, names the conclusion each gap weakens, and distinguishes evidence-backed assets, assets to check, and assets the sources cannot assess.
- [ ] **Pivot accuracy:** Each important pivot names its view or filter, conversation, source capture, and purpose. No filter is treated as collection proof, and no packet pattern is treated as process identity.
- [ ] **Reproducible evidence:** Original and derivative IDs differ, and each derivative ID links back to its parent original ID. The index records filenames, hashes, origin, coverage, handling, changes, and recipients. Packet claims cite the original capture ID and hash, original frame number and timestamp, useful stream context, and a locating Wireshark view or filter—not screenshots alone.
- [ ] **Careful chronology:** Timeline entries retain comparable displayed times in a stated common time zone and format, original timestamps, source references, clock uncertainty, and gaps. Proximity is not presented as causation without evidence.
- [ ] **Reusable decisions:** The playbook has required sources, Wireshark views or filters and the evidence each pivot seeks, harmless and suspicious branches, a stop condition, and an explicit escalate, close, or request-evidence outcome.
- [ ] **Testable detection:** The proposal identifies behavior, telemetry, fields, dependencies, comparison, false positives, misses, and local threshold validation. An unperformed test is `untested`, not successful.
- [ ] **Visible improvement and skill plan:** The revision log preserves earlier wording and explains the change. The skill plan targets a demonstrated weakness, includes a small exercise on authorized material and a measurable progress check, names needed telemetry, the question each source answers, and its access authorizer, and identifies a learner-chosen next case rather than a course-assigned scenario.
- [ ] **Safe sharing:** Derivatives, attachments, metadata, secrets, mappings, removals, and recipients receive authorized human review. Inspect retained packets for sensitive contents, metadata, and embedded decryption secrets, as well as screenshots and attachments; consistently replace unnecessary names, addresses, and personal details, protect the mapping for authorized tracing, and record removals and permitted recipients. Selecting fewer packets does not sanitize them.

Expected structural result: every check is supportable before packaging. This does not validate case truth, collection completeness, authorization, sanitization, or detection efficacy.
