# Define the Pivots and Stop Conditions — answer guide

**Scope:** No case answer exists here. Keep inventory values and the evidence ledger empty.

## Reasoning key

1. **Starting question:** Support requires relevant host-to-peer activity inside covered time plus a reason the routine explanation falls short. Weakening requires trustworthy host-and-time-specific scheduling or asset records. Unresolved means coverage or context cannot distinguish the explanations. A plausible story is not confirmation.
2. **Pivots:** Host checks outbound traffic and replies and justifies expansion. Time uses the reported period and surrounding traffic with reasoned boundaries. Protocol separates decoded evidence from port inference and notes encryption or incomplete-exchange limits. Peer declares filtered or broader scope and identifies who can confirm business purpose.
3. **Tools and evidence:** A display filter changes the view, not the capture. Conversations measures help select candidates, not verdicts. A packet citation needs source file, displayed timestamp and time basis, and frame. Its limited claim is an exchange observed at the capture point—not process, intent, authorization, compromise, or technique. Its timestamp does not validate clock accuracy.
4. **Stopping:** Concern prompts proportionate inquiry. Confirmed routine context weakens suspicion while limits remain documented. An evidence gap prompts a specific request and an unresolved decision. Protocol familiarity, a reply, an empty filtered view, and packet count do not settle the question.

## Checkable expected result

A complete worksheet has three planning answers; four pivot entries naming a question, hidden context, and challenging result; a planned Conversations scope; and a criterion plus next step for all three outcomes. It invents no case values, leaves the ledger empty, and keeps observation, explanation, and decision separate.
