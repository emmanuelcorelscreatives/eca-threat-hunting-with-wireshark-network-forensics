# Answer guide

Combined capture has75 frames and streams0–4. No-secrets and C/D-log outputs record exact executed results. No secrets reveals no HTTP. With C/D log, D request `/academy/case-D` is decoded at frame50; its full response is not decoded after the deleted segment. A and B remain encrypted; C remains unusable for HTTP despite a matching log because its captured start is missing. Matching C/D secrets are positively verified against their complete controls, rather than assumed from a selected path. E RSA control on TShark4.2.2 with GnuTLS decodes `/academy/case-E` at70 and response200 at71. Linux4.4 without GnuTLS cannot perform this RSA control; do not claim it did.

A ServerHello6 selects0x0304 and suite0x1302. An RSA certificate authentication key is not the TLS1.3 traffic secret. B ServerHello23 selects0xc02f, ECDHE_RSA; its ephemeral key establishment prevents decryption by this certificate key, and supplied log has no matching B random. C lacks its handshake and TCP opening frames; no selected version can be derived from absent ServerHello in this derivative. The separate complete control and collection receipt identify the experiment only. A fuller capture is the next request. D starts at41, ServerHello46, request50; combined54 marks a gap after original D14 was removed. Other records, such as request50, remain usable; do not describe the whole connection as decrypted or unreadable. E has ServerHello66 selecting0x002f and ClientKeyExchange68; fresh RSA key transport plus matching private key supports the actual successful control. Missing ClientKeyExchange or resumption would remove that basis.

An empty log field selects no log; it proves neither absence of secrets elsewhere nor that all traffic is undecryptable. A selected path proves neither matching secrets nor successful application dissection. Empty HTTP can reflect wrong/missing secrets, absent handshake, missing TCP data, decoding, reassembly or absence of HTTP. Inspect these before deciding. Preferences are under Edit → Preferences → Protocols → TLS; RSA key support depends on the build. QUIC has different transport/dissection and handshake requirements, so do not transpose this TCP checklist unchanged.

Model case note: In Academy D, stream3 has a captured TLS1.3 ServerHello46. With the authorized D log, request50 decodes. At54 a TCP sequence gap follows the deliberately omitted segment, and the complete response is not decoded. The separate complete control confirms the expected response but does not restore evidence to the derivative. Confidence is high in this designed omission; a real case would require a fuller capture or endpoint evidence. Packets alone do not establish user, process, authorization or intent.
