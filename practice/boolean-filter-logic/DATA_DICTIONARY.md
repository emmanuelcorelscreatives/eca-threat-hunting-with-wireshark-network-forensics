# Fixture, manifest, fields, and evidence dictionary

## What is authored; what is packaged

`executed-fixture-spec.json` is the **actual** version-1 synthetic input to the current packaged `offline-builder.py`; byte-identical `fixture-spec.json` is an alias, not a second executed input. Its ordered `events` author Ethernet/IPv4 frames with ISO 8601 UTC `timestamp`, transport `kind`, documentation-address `src`/`dst`, and `sport`/`dport`. For TCP, `flags`, `seq`, and `ack` author connection/close progression with **zero payload**; the two UDP events also have no payload. A=`192.0.2.10`, B=`198.51.100.20`, C=`203.0.113.30`. All are reserved documentation IPv4 addresses. The output `practice.pcap` is PCAP, not the PCAPNG in an older draft. The **current** builder creates an output exclusively and refuses an existing file; the archived original builder had overwrite behavior. A fresh current-builder run produced byte-identical `practice.pcap`, SHA-256 `a39a5ab5c482a49eb62652f502e7cdc8ea8de34294edb19309cbd8a1af03619c`. The verification copy is not another capture scenario. No real collector, historical event, observation point, or application activity was generated.

`packet-manifest.csv` has **20 authored rows**. `designOrdinal` means event order, **not** an observed frame reference. `timestampUTC`, `src`, `dst`, `transport`, `srcPort`, `dstPort`, and `tcpFlags` are design values; blank `tcpFlags` means authored UDP. `scenarioLabel` is an authored teaching label, not telemetry. `pair443WindowDesign` and `oneHost443WindowDesign` are predicted Boolean memberships for TCP port 443 in `[12:00:00, 12:05:00)` UTC on 2026-01-01, with included start and excluded end. `expected-results.json` likewise stores predicted *design ordinals*. Its `observedCounts` and `observedFrames` remain null and its `DESIGN_ONLY_NOT_EXECUTED`/release-gate text predates the completed filter checks. **Do not reinterpret null JSON fields as measured results or silently edit the source files.**

`practice.pcap` is the **binary measured input**. `all-frames.stdout.txt` supplies decoded `frame.number` 1–20, aligned here with event order, plus `frame.time_epoch` (numeric epoch **seconds**) and `frame.time_utc` (human-readable UTC-labelled time), `frame.len`, `frame.cap_len`, `frame.protocols`, IPv4 addresses, TCP ports, `tcp.stream`, `tcp.seq_raw`, `tcp.ack_raw`, `tcp.len`, and payload/DNS/HTTP columns. The PCAP precision is **microseconds** even when rendered with nine fractional places. `frame.time_iso8601` was unsupported in the recorded source-built Linux 4.4.0 inventory and was not extracted. `frame.len` and `frame.cap_len` measure captured/on-wire **frame bytes**, not application data. `tcp.len` measures TCP payload bytes and is **zero** in every logged TCP row; its UDP `tcp.*` cells are blank, not zero-valued TCP measurements. Raw TCP seq/ack fields differ from a GUI's potentially relative numbers; SYN and FIN each consume one authored sequence number. `tcp.stream` and conversation grouping do not prove an application transaction.

The **original** `all-frames` extraction did not include UDP ports, TCP flags, UDP payload-length fields, or checksum-status fields. The later real `linux-command-15.stdout.txt` and corrected UTF-8 `windows-command-15.stdout.txt` do include `udp.srcport`, `udp.dstport`, and `tcp.flags.str`. They show UDP **53000→443** on frame 10 and **443→53000** on frame 11, with no TCP flags on those UDP rows; the TCP flag strings distinguish SYN, SYN/ACK, ACK, and FIN/ACK packets. `windows-utf8-decoder-correction.json` pertains to character decoding of the flag string, not changed packet bytes. Use a *per-packet* Flags section or a measured `tcp.flags.syn` value to say whether a specific packet has SYN set. In the native Windows details frame 2 reads `Syn: Set`; frames 3 and 17 read `Syn: Not set` (frame 17 reads `Fin: Set`). Their TCP pane's `[Conversation completeness] SYN: Present` refers to the conversation and does **not** override those per-packet bits. The copied native detail texts may contain expanded rows outside their accompanying screenshots' visible viewport. Native packet-detail and time-menu records await independent whole-kit assessment.

`measured-filter-results.json` records **14 actual accepted filter expressions**, `actualFrameNumbers`, exit codes, and corresponding `linux-command-NN`/`windows-command-NN` stdout/stderr filenames for this same PCAP. The two platforms selected the same frames. `windows-current-gui-execution.json` and `windows-gui-boolean-*.png` record actual File → Open, exact-filter application, and Clear views; `windows-independent-gui-batch-{5,6,7,8,9}.json` independently passes the **filter-view** screenshots. This is stronger and newer evidence than the earlier logs' pending-filter prose, but it is not independent approval of the new expanded packet details or the whole kit. Validation-only `boolean-practice.pcap`/`fields-practice.pcap` GUI source aliases are byte-identical to `practice.pcap`; they neither add datasets nor change the learner filename.

`capture-info.stdout.txt` describes the PCAP file, microsecond precision, and its **earliest/latest packets**, not the duration or accuracy of an actual collection. `protocol-hierarchy.stdout.txt` counts protocol frames and **frame bytes**; `tcp-conversations.stdout.txt` and `ip-conversations.stdout.txt` count conversation frames and **frame bytes**, not transferred-file bytes. Empty `malformed.stdout.txt` is the executed `_ws.malformed` query only. Blank TCP payload, DNS, and HTTP fields, empty-payload authored UDP, and no validated application content cannot establish DNS/HTTP requests, TLS, files, process activity, or maliciousness. Native checksum statuses shown for inspected packets are **Unverified**, not proof of validated checksums.
