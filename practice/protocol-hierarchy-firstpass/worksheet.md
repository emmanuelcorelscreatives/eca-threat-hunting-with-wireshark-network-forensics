# First-pass triage worksheet

Use your **authorized working copy**. Indicate whether it is the labelled synthetic Academy fixture or a separately supplied narrated-case capture; never mix their frame references or statistics. The lecture uses Windows 11 / Wireshark 4.4.0; the recorded companion lab uses native Linux Qt Wireshark 4.4.0 on Ubuntu 24.04.5 LTS. Windows reproduction is optional and not claimed executed. Record the platform of your own observations. The supplied Linux GUI images are distinct from CLI measurements.

1. **Scope before statistics.** Working-copy name and, for the fixture, checked SHA-256 ___; scenario asset and proposed role ___; capture point ___; UTC start/end ___; read filter or load exclusions (write `none` only if checked) ___; display filter ___; statistics limited to displayed packets? ___; coverage limits ___. In the GUI use **File → Open**, leave the read filter empty where possible, and check the display-filter bar. The CSV is authored context, not packet-derived ownership or authorization. A workstation-side capture does not show all unrelated unicast traffic on a switched network.

2. **Map the broad mix first.** Open **Statistics → Protocol Hierarchy**. Displayed-filter area in that newly opened window ___; broad decoded families and row names with their actual measures ___ . Does the mix fit the documented workstation, resolver, service endpoint, and network-device roles in this window? ___ . The hierarchy is a scoped map of decoded packets, not a list of distinct applications or an authorization finding. For a selected row, write its exact name ___; named measure (**Packets**, **Bytes**, **End Packets**, or **PDUs**) ___; value and scope from *your* view ___ . Packets can count at multiple layers; row percentages are not additive. End Packets means the last dissected layer; PDUs count protocol data units and can outnumber packets. Child totals need not equal a parent because of reassembly, transport overhead, or data with no higher-layer dissection. Neither a small category nor a large one is an incident verdict; do not turn rows into a pie chart.

3. **Pause before packet evidence.** **Observation:** This scoped summary decoded ___ in row ___ with measure ___ . **Hypothesis:** It might not fit asset ___ during this window because ___ . **Alternatives that could change your mind:** backup, monitoring, expected service, troubleshooting, a network-device function, or incorrect dissection ___ . **Packet evidence: ___ [LEAVE BLANK UNTIL CHECKED].** Rarity alone is not the hypothesis; unknown asset ownership or coverage is an uncertainty, not permission to label hostile activity.

4. **Test the lead against packets.** Close the hierarchy, enter the chosen protocol in the display-filter bar, and apply it. `dns` is the lecture's example expression: DNS is present in the synthetic fixture, but its presence in the unavailable narrated case is **not** established. If the filter is empty, check expression and load/display scope; mark the packet check **unresolved** if no usable match remains. For a match, record a frame number from *your* copy ___; IP direction ___; decoded layers and relevant fields ___; whether the details support or challenge the row label ___ . In Packet Details distinguish bracketed/generated deductions from captured bytes. If the label and details disagree, keep incorrect decoding open as an alternative. A TCP destination port of 80 alone does not establish HTTP or file content.

5. **Compare scopes deliberately.** While the chosen filter is in force, close and reopen **Protocol Hierarchy**; record the reopened window's displayed-filter area ___, the statistics-scope choice ___, and only the values you actually measure ___ . Clear the display filter; close and reopen the hierarchy *again*; verify its filter area ___ and record the broader loaded-view values ___ . An already-open window may retain old statistics. State which comparison is filtered and which is broader ___ . A display filter changes the loaded view, not the capture file; clearing it cannot recover packets missed during collection or excluded during loading.

6. **Assessment and next action.** Packet evidence now checked or explicitly unresolved ___ . Plausible routine or decoding explanation ___; assessment and confidence limit ___; asset-owner question and needed capture-coverage or other telemetry ___ . Report the observed mix, exact scope, row, named measure, and packet reference—not a threat label. If ownership or purpose cannot be settled, write **inconclusive**. A missing protocol here is not proof of network-wide absence.
