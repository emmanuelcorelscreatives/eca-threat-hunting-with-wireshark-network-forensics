## Checked reasoning and uncertainty

Follow TCP presents captured bytes in expected sequence order. That is a reading aid; the original packet rows preserve the recorded sequence and times for context. In this fixture, captured TCP contributions alternate in frames 2, 3, 4 and 5, and the authored sequence positions advance without an intentionally repeated payload. The key does not assert a retransmission occurred. If another capture shows apparent repeated data or Wireshark labels a packet as a retransmission, inspect sequence numbers, both directions, timing and capture coverage before diagnosing network loss or suspicious behavior. A label alone does not establish a cause; a packet absent at one observation point may have reached another system.

The first visible TCP content is `side-A: amber` in frame 2, and the last is `side-B: green` in frame 5. The file begins its visible TCP exchange with payload and supplies no SYN, FIN or RST. These observations establish the file's visible boundaries, not the real conversation's beginning or ending. A capture beginning mid-conversation, missing packets or a missing direction are possibilities to test, not established findings. Protected content can explain unreadability in other captures, but these supplied marker payloads are readable; do not claim encryption was observed here.

UDP provides neither delivery guarantees nor protection from duplicate datagrams. An absent direction does not distinguish a datagram never sent from one not captured. The supplied fixture contains four authored datagrams in alternating directions, with no authored duplicate. This does not prove reception or exclude unseen duplicates outside this file.
