# Packet verification worksheet

Question: Does each automated row describe a packet in its stated source and time window, and what context does the row omit? Use the actual supplied exports, not just hypothetical-claims.csv. The latter retains the original H1–H4 hypotheses for comparison.

1. Record the installed GUI/TShark/mergecap versions, profile, time display basis and source hashes. Copy the source ID, capture point and measured coverage from manifest.json. Is the open file a source or a derivative? Resolve mismatches before drawing findings.
2. Open sensor-a. Record captured total and displayed total without a filter; apply dns and record the displayed count. Compare its two exported DNS rows with the two IPv4 endpoint rows in a-endpoints-dns.stdout.txt. Explain why packet and endpoint units differ even when the row totals happen to match. Compare a-stats-unfiltered.stdout.txt with a-stats-dns.stdout.txt. Check the separate statistic filter.
3. Select sensor-a-response.tsv frame 8. Open sensor-a and use Go → Go to Packet → 8. Expand HTTP in Packet Details. Record time, direction, response code and both Set-Cookie occurrences. Compare a-first-cookie.stdout.txt with the all-occurrence export. Check absent http.request.method and dns.qry.name cells; do empty cells mean absent packets?
4. Inspect source-a frame 6 (request), frames 7–9 (nearby context), and the DNS selection. Record what a narrow filter hides. Packet contents establish observed fields in this synthetic file, not purpose, operator identity or suspicious intent.
5. Open sensor-b separately. Check its response frames 9 and 16. Compare the first response with sensor-a frame 8; compare the later response at 09:00:00.350 UTC with sensor-a's coverage. Are the source frame numbers interchangeable? Does an out-of-window response establish loss by sensor-a? Keep both source references.
6. Open captures/merged.pcapng and consult mapping/merged-frames.csv. Select a derivative frame, find the checked original source/frame candidate, open that original and compare its exact timestamp and packet fields. Explain the mapping's byte-and-timestamp rule and its unresolved-multiple-match behavior. Record derivative and original references separately. Merge overlap is not an instruction to delete packets.
7. Compare a-readlimit-one.stdout.txt with the whole-file HTTP export. What does -c 1 limit? Align input, window, profile, filter, read limit, counted unit and transformations before comparing counts. Explain why a-statistic row is not a packet-list row.
8. Compare a varied range of request, repeated-field response, DNS and second-source rows against packets. Separate packet observations, automated claims and untested explanations in your note. Keep original timestamps; measured real clock offset is unknown. Review repeat-run export equivalence and execute the read-only verifier. Record differences, confidence limits and the smallest next check. Retain the transcript, manifest and note together.
